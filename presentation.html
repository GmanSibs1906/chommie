Part 1 · 20 seconds · start here

Four pain points

Teachers have four big problems.

One: marking takes over seven hours a week.

Two: planning the term and every lesson takes hours more.

Three: once the plan is made, they follow it as is. They can't see who is falling behind, so the plan never changes to fit the class.

Four: every learner gets the same lesson. Nothing is made to help each learner with what they struggle with.

Source if asked: South African teachers mark about 7.1 hours a week, against an OECD average of 4.6 (TALIS 2024).

Part 2 · 40 seconds

Meet Chommie

Meet Chommie: a digital colleague for teachers. Chommie means friend.

Here is what makes it different. Chommie builds the whole term plan from the official ATP. It knows every topic and every test date. Then, as the marks come in, the plan changes. If the class is struggling with a topic, Chommie adds a short re-teach. But it stays on record: it never drops a required topic, and it never misses a test date.

At the same time, every learner gets their own homework, made from exactly what they got wrong. In a class of thirty-four, twenty learners can get twenty different homework sheets.

And none of this adds work for the teacher. She takes photos with her phone. Chommie does the rest. Better for the teacher, better for the learners. A win-win.

Part 3 · about 2 minutes 15

Live demo

Talk while you tap. If something is slow, keep talking: "Two AIs are reading the page right now."

a · Set up a class (20 seconds)

  1. Tap Create class. Name: 6B. Grade 6. Subject Mathematics. Save.

Setting up takes a minute. I make my class: 6B, Grade 6, Maths. A teacher can have many classes, and each class keeps its own plan, learners, marks and homework.

b · Load the ATP (25 seconds)

  1. Open ATP. Pick Grade 6, Mathematics, Term 2. Tap Load this ATP.
  2. When the topics show (Common fractions), scroll a little, then tap Approve this ATP.

Now the plan. I don't type anything. Chommie has the official DBE teaching plans for every grade, from Grade 1 to 12. A school can also upload its own PDF or a photo. Chommie reads the weeks, the topics and the test dates, and puts every topic on a real school day.

c · Add learners from a photo (25 seconds)

  1. Go back to the class and open Learners. Under Photo of a class list, tap Take or choose a photo and pick Names.jpg.
  2. Show the names it read. Tap Add these 4 learners.

No typing here either. This is my class list, written by hand. I take a photo, and Chommie reads the names, even the long surnames like "van der Merwe" and "du Plessis". The teacher checks them and taps confirm.

d · Mark the books (45 seconds)

  1. Go back to the class and open Mark. For each learner, tap Take photo and pick their page: Thabo.jpg, Lerato.jpg, Sipho.jpg. The next learner comes up by itself.
  2. Tap Mark 3 books. While it works, hold up the real paper and say the lines below.

Now the part teachers hate most: marking. This is a real page from a learner's book. Handwritten fractions.

I pick the learner, take a photo, and the next learner is ready. Before the photo leaves the phone, the learner's name is blacked out. The AI never sees a child's name.

Two AI markers, from two different companies, Anthropic and Google, mark every answer on their own. If they agree, good. If they don't, a third AI from OpenAI re-reads that answer.

e · Caught & fixed: Chommie checks itself (25 seconds)

  1. Tap the green badge at the top: ✓ Caught & fixed.
  2. Tap a photo under Waiting for you. Then tap Whole page. Close.

This is my favourite part. Chommie shows where it caught itself. Here the markers did not agree, or the handwriting was not clear. So Chommie did not guess. It cut out the answer and is waiting for me, the teacher, to decide.

AI that says "I'm not sure" is AI a teacher can trust.

f · Heat map and homework (20 seconds)

  1. Open Heat map. Then open Homework.

Every learner, every topic, at a glance. Green: got it. Amber: shaky. Red: struggling.

Here is the rule. If forty percent or more of the class is struggling with a topic, Chommie re-plans and adds a short re-teach, still before the test. If it's only a few learners, personal homework fixes it. Each learner gets a printed sheet on exactly what they got wrong. And when the homework comes back, I photograph it, and it is marked against that learner's own answers.

Then the loop runs again, every week, until every square is green before the exam.

If the Wi-Fi or an AI fails: say "Let me show you the run we recorded this morning" and play the demo video, or tap Load demo run on the Classes page. Don't apologise twice. Move on.

Part 4 · 45 seconds

One loop. Seven agents.

  • Switch to the landing page tab at #agents. Let the loop light up.

Behind this are seven agents. Each one has one job.

The Coordinator runs the weekly loop and decides the next step. The Curriculum Reader reads the ATP and the class list. The Term Planner puts every topic on a real school day. The Lesson Writer writes the lessons and each learner's homework. Two Markers mark every book. The Reviewer checks the markers. And the Analyst builds the heat map. Then the loop starts again.

And how do we stop the AI from making things up? Four ways. One: the planner, the dates and the totals are plain code, not AI. Two: every memo answer is proven by calculation. Three: two AIs from different companies mark, and a third checks them. Four: if Chommie is not sure, it asks the teacher. It never guesses. The teacher approves everything.

Part 5 · 35 seconds

Why it works in real life

We kept it simple on purpose. One workflow: the weekly teaching loop. One owner: the teacher. The Head of Department sees the overview. And one number that matters: how many learners move from red to green, with the ATP finished on time.

It works on the phone a teacher already has. Learners don't need devices: their homework is on paper. Names are hidden before anything is sent, and photos are deleted once the marks are confirmed.

Our next step is a thirty-day pilot: one school, one grade, one subject.

Judges' questions

What if the AI marks something wrong?

Two AIs from different companies mark every answer. If they disagree, a third re-reads it. If it is still not clear, the teacher decides. Nothing goes out until the teacher approves. You saw it on the Caught & fixed page.

What about messy handwriting?

Chommie flags it and shows the teacher a picture of that answer. It never guesses. Other tools say "even messy handwriting works". We think a teacher should decide.

Is learner data safe? What about POPIA?

The name strip is blacked out on the phone before any photo is sent. Photos are deleted after the marks are confirmed. A real pilot needs parent consent first, and today's demo uses only made-up learners.

Other apps already mark homework. What is new?

Marking is only one step. Chommie plans the whole term from the ATP, re-plans when the class struggles without missing a test date, gives every learner their own printed homework, and marks that homework against their own answers. It is a full loop, not just a marking tool.

What about learners without phones or laptops?

They don't need one. Only the teacher uses a phone. Learners get paper.

Does the teacher lose control?

No. Chommie drafts, the teacher decides. The teacher can change any lesson, any homework sheet and both numbers in the 40% rule.

Which grades and subjects work?

The library has the official DBE ATPs for Grades 1 to 12. Our demo is Grade 6 Maths, where answers can be checked by calculation. The pilot starts with one grade and one subject.

How did you build it so fast?

With Albert, our AI team. It researched, planned, designed and built Chommie, and I approved each step. That is what Albert does.
